operator notices and a distracted one doesn't (scenario S-23).

3.7 Core physics model
----------------------
Real-time 3D multigroup space-time kinetics: hexagonal-Z nodal diffusion, solved with the improved
quasi-static method and coupled assembly by assembly to the thermal-hydraulics. Every value in
§3.1–3.6, plus the peaking factors in §2.4, is a target the model must reproduce, not an input to
it.

NOTE: Why multigroup, not two-group. Two-group diffusion is the standard for light-water reactor
      simulators, where neutrons split cleanly into fast and thermal. A sodium fast reactor has
      almost no thermal neutrons, so its realistic equivalent is several fast energy groups.

What 3D adds in this core: a single rod moving tilts the flux and the thermocouple map; sodium
voiding adds reactivity at the core centre but removes it in the outer rings; a blocked assembly
changes local power; a gas bubble's reactivity depends on its path. Whole-core power response
changes little, because a fast core is tightly coupled. That is also why real sodium fast reactor
safety codes often still use point kinetics.

(Item / Specification)
Method                : Time-dependent multigroup neutron diffusion in hexagonal-Z geometry,
                        factorised by the improved quasi-static (IQS) method: flux = amplitude(t) ×
                        shape(r, E, t)
Energy groups         : 4 †, raised to 6 once performance allows. No upscattering in a fast
                        spectrum, so groups are solved in sequence from fastest to slowest
Delayed neutrons      : 6 precursor groups in every fuel node, constants from §3.1
Radial mesh           : One node per hexagonal position in rings 0–12 (469 positions: fuel, rods,
                        reflector); albedo boundary outside ring 12 †
Axial mesh            : 14 layers: 2 lower reflector, 10 fuel at 100 mm each, 2 upper plenum and
                        structure †
Size                  : 469 × 14 × 4 = 26,264 flux unknowns, plus precursors in the fuel nodes
Amplitude step        : Every 0.1 s: prompt-jump point kinetics (§14.2) using reactivity, β_eff and
                        Λ recomputed from the latest shape, weighted by the adjoint flux
Shape step            : Every 2 s at steady power; every 0.5 s while reactivity changes faster than
                        5 pcm/s, and for 60 s after any trip †. Time-sliced across frames
Steady state          : Eigenvalue (k_eff) and adjoint solve at server start, after a reset, and on
                        demand for the ECP calculator
Rods                  : The rod tip splits its node into absorber and sodium-follower fractions by
                        volume, with a cusping correction
Doppler               : Each fuel node's absorption and fission cross sections vary with ln(T_fuel)
                        of that node
Sodium density        : Each node's cross sections vary linearly with that node's sodium density, so
                        voiding happens node by node
Axial fuel expansion  : Fuel node height grows with fuel temperature; number densities fall to match
Radial expansion      : Diagrid (core inlet temperature) and load pads (core outlet temperature)
                        scale the assembly pitch; cross sections scale with density
Drivelines and vessel : Offsets added to every rod's effective position, with the §3.2 lags
Burnup                : Every fuel assembly tracks its own burnup; cross sections interpolate
                        between tabulated burnup points
Detectors             : PR quadrant chambers and SR and WR channels read weighted sums of peripheral
                        node fluxes, so quadrant tilt is physical
Thermocouple map      : Each outlet temperature comes from that assembly's own nodal power and flow
                        (§2.5)
Fallback              : Point kinetics with lumped feedback (§3.2) stays in the code as a low-load
                        fallback and as a cross-check during development
