====================================================================================================
0. HOW TO READ THIS SPEC
====================================================================================================
Every number here is a starting value meant to agree with every other number. Values marked † are tuning
values, and a few plant-level figures are rounded (the steam generator section duty, for one). §1.1 is the
canonical thermal power; everything else derives from it. Appendix C lists what Rev A4 corrected. The heat balance was
computed with IAPWS-IF97 steam tables and the Fink–Leibowitz sodium correlations, with turbine
losses set so efficiency lands between BN-600 and BN-800. Physics values sit inside the published
ranges for BN-600, BN-800, Superphénix, PFR and EFR. It is not a licensing-grade design and doesn't
need to be.


0.1 Conventions
---------------
(Item / Convention)
Pressure     : Absolute, MPa or kPa, unless marked g
Temperature  : °C; temperature differences in K
Reactivity   : pcm (10⁻⁵ Δk/k); $1 = 360 pcm
Rod position : mm withdrawn from fully inserted, 0–1,100 mm
Power        : %FP of 2,380 MWt; MWe gross unless marked net
†            : Tuning value: realistic in size, meant to change in playtesting


0.2 Clocks
----------
* Physics
    Rate: ×1 real time, always
    Drives: Kinetics, thermal-hydraulics, pumps, valves, electrical, protection
* Low-power acceleration †
    Rate: ×10, Modes 3–5 only, no P1/P2 alarms, all manned desks agree; also allowed in a
      station blackout once the reactor is confirmed subcritical
    Drives: Heatup, cooldown, long waits in blackout scenarios
* Grid clock †
    Rate: ×12 (one grid day = 2 h real)
    Drives: Dispatcher schedule, demand curve, weather and sea, LCO completion times
* Slow processes †
    Rate: ×360 of real time
    Drives: Burnup, impurity ingress, cold trap loading, hydrogen drift, heater failures.
      Fuel and tube degradation (§12.2, §12.4) run on the grid clock, not here

Ramp limits and response windows are real time unless marked grid time.

1.1 Plant at a glance
---------------------
(Parameter / Value)
Station            : Kallskär Nuclear Power Plant, unit K1, Hälsingland coast, Sweden
Reactor            : SFR-1000: pool-type sodium-cooled fast reactor, MOX fuel
Thermal power      : 2,380 MWt
Gross output       : 1,000 MWe at the 5.0 kPa rating point (2 × 500 MWe); up to ≈ 1,028 MWe with
                     cold winter seawater
Net output         : ≈ 935 MWe (house load ≈ 65 MWe: main feed pumps 19.6, primary pumps 10.3,
                     secondary pumps 6.2, circulating water 14.8, everything else ≈ 14)
Gross efficiency   : 42.0%
Primary circuit    : 1,250 t sodium, 3 pumps, 6 IHXs, core 375 → 550 °C, 10,694 kg/s
Secondary circuit  : 3 loops, 3,112 kg/s each, 320 → 520 °C, ≈ 400 t sodium each
Steam generators   : 3 × 8 sections (evaporator, superheater, reheater), 99.5 MWt per section
Steam at SG outlet : 14.0 MPa, 505 °C; reheat 2.6 MPa, 505 °C; feedwater 240 °C
Main steam flow    : 291.7 kg/s per loop; 437.5 kg/s per turbine
Turbines           : 2 × HP + IP + 2 LP, 3,000 rpm, condenser 5.0 kPa, seawater cooled
Core               : 301 fuel assemblies, 30 rod positions, 1.0 m active height, ≈ 29 tHM
Kinetics           : Real-time 3D multigroup space-time kinetics (§3.7); β_eff 360 pcm; Λ 4.0 × 10⁻⁷
                     s; no xenon effect
Decay heat removal : 4 DRACS loops × 20 MWt, natural circulation
Emergency power    : 4 EDGs × 6.3 MW


1.2 Topology
------------
PLANT TOPOLOGY (text version of the diagram)

Reactor pool, primary sodium (1,250 t):
  Core 2,380 MWt -> hot pool 550 °C -> IHX 1A/1B, IHX 2A/2B, IHX 3A/3B -> cold pool 375 °C
  -> primary pumps PP1, PP2, PP3 -> diagrid -> core

Secondary sodium and main steam:
  Loop 1: IHX 1A/1B -> SG1 (8 sections) -> MSIV-1 -> Header A -> Turbine A (500 MWe)
  Loop 3: IHX 3A/3B -> SG3 (8 sections) -> MSIV-3 -> Header B -> Turbine B (500 MWe)
  Loop 2: IHX 2A/2B -> SG2 (8 sections) -> splitter
            branch A: CV-2A -> NRV-2A -> Header A
            branch B: CV-2B -> NRV-2B -> Header B

Reheat (cold reheat from each turbine's HP exhaust, hot reheat back to the same turbine's IP):
  Turbine A: reheater modules of SG1 sections 1-8 and SG2 sections 1-4
  Turbine B: reheater modules of SG3 sections 1-8 and SG2 sections 5-8

1.4 Site
--------
(Item / Choice)
Location            : Kallskär, a former skerry on the Hälsingland coast of the Bothnian Sea,
                      Sweden, about 61.5° N. Land uplift joined it to the mainland long ago; the
                      name stayed
Bidding zone        : SE2. Power flows south to the load centres in SE3 across transfer limits, so
                      grid control sometimes holds the plant back
Ground              : Precambrian granite and gneiss; very low seismicity
Sea                 : Brackish, about 5 psu; no tide worth modelling; sea level swings about ±0.5 m
                      with wind and air pressure
Ice                 : Fast ice in the archipelago from January to April in a normal winter; frazil
                      ice at the intake on cold, clear nights before the ice forms
Cooling water       : Intake canal on the north side drawing from about 10 m depth; outfall to the
                      south so warm water doesn't recirculate by accident; a recirculation gate
                      sends some back on purpose for de-icing
Land uplift         : About 7 mm a year, so the intake canal is dredged every decade or so
Layout              : Reactor building on bedrock at the centre; SG buildings set apart for sodium
                      fire separation; turbine hall on the seaward side; switchyard inland; four EDG
                      buildings in separated divisions; backup control room on the landward side.
                      The headland is a natural map boundary
Time zone, daylight : CET/CEST; about 6 h of daylight in December, about 19 h in June


1.5 History
-----------
Sweden never built a fast reactor. Everything below is alternate history.

(Year / Event)
1974 : After the oil crisis, a Swedish utility consortium studies a sodium fast reactor to stretch
       uranium supplies
1978 : Kallskär chosen for its bedrock, cold deep water and a nearby 400 kV line. Kallskär
       Kraftgrupp AB (KKG) is formed to build and run it
1980 : Neutral Sweden licenses from both sides: pool and core from the French fast reactor
       programme, sectional steam generators from the Soviet BN-600. The joint design office works
       in English, and so does the control room to this day
1981 : Construction starts. Both 500 MWe turbine-generators are ordered full size, on the
       expectation of three loops
1988 : First criticality. Loop 1 and turbine A enter service; the reactor is limited to one-third
       power
1991 : Loop 3 and turbine B enter service
1996 : SG2 arrives after years of fabrication problems. Both turbines already exist, so loop 2 is
       split between them through the splitter
2009 : Modernisation: variable-speed pump drives, digital protection and plant computer, new
       hydrogen meters. Screens join the original analog panels (§10.6)
2026 : 38 years in service. Swedish licences have no fixed end date, so the next milestone is the
       ten-yearly periodic safety review in 2028

1.7 Seasons
-----------
The season follows the real-world calendar by default; a private server owner can pin one.

* Winter, Dec–Mar
    Grid: Cold snaps drive peak demand and maximum-output instructions
    Sea and weather: Sea ice; frazil ice on cold clear nights; wet snow and ice on lines; aurora
      nights
    Plant effects: Best vacuum, ≈ 514 MWe per unit; intake icing (S-24); line trips (S-27, S-28);
      geomagnetic storms (S-26)
* Spring, Apr–Jun
    Grid: Snowmelt floods northern hydro: transfer-limit curtailments, sometimes negative prices
    Sea and weather: Ice break-up, drift ice
    Plant effects: Frequent down-ramps and fatigue; drift ice on intake screens
* Summer, Jul–Aug
    Grid: Lowest demand; outage season
    Sea and weather: Warmest water; upwelling can drop intake temperature 5–10 K in a day; algae and
      drift weed
    Plant effects: Worst vacuum, down to the 500 MWe rating; screen fouling (S-25)
* Autumn, Sep–Nov
    Grid: Demand climbing; variable wind
    Sea and weather: Storms; sea level swings
    Plant effects: Line trips; frazil ice risk from November
